A complex world.

End-to-end digital design for RAD, bringing technical content, brand expression and mobile into one visual language.

THE PROBLEM

Make a technical website
feel connected.

The project brought together a large website and mobile experience. My work connected the client’s requirements with a refreshed digital design language and a more modern presentation of communication products.

The design needed room for technical content, use cases and articles, while keeping a recognizable identity across the experience.

THE SOLUTION

A clearer way in.
A consistent way through.

I brought the experience together through a shared design language, search in the homepage hero, and distinct treatments for use cases and editorial resources.

The result is a connected set of website and mobile designs: recognizable in their visual language, specific in how they present different kinds of content.

THE DESIGN PROCESS

The screens are only
part of the story.

The Figma workflow brings the homepage, interaction states and responsive layouts into one place. It shows how the experience holds together across desktop, tablet and mobile, and how individual moments fit into the wider page.

Alongside the main layouts, the file includes search suggestions, contact validation, sending and confirmation states. These details carry the design beyond a single polished screen.

DESIGN DECISION / DISCOVERY

Put search
in the first view.

The homepage pairs its brand message with a prominent search field. In the typing state, results appear directly beneath it and identify whether an item is a product, solution or blog entry.

This gives the visitor a direct route into the content alongside the main navigation and the homepage’s category cards.

DESIGN DECISION / VISUAL LANGUAGE

Technical content,
with a human presence.

I developed a visual language that connects the website’s content with the brand. Warm gradients, violet typography, flowing lines and rounded surfaces give the design a consistent foundation.

That language extends beyond the hero. In the homepage’s company section, photography and a process timeline bring people and production into the story.

DESIGN DECISION / CONTENT HIERARCHY

Different content.
Different cues.

I gave use cases and articles distinct visual treatments while keeping the same underlying card rhythm.

Use cases use branded graphic covers and concise titles. Insights introduce imagery, resource icons and short summaries. Category labels help distinguish the content before a visitor opens it.

INTERACTION STATES

Design the response,
as well as the request.

The contact flow includes more than the form itself. The validation design places messages next to the fields that need attention, while the sent state confirms receipt and sets an expectation for the response.

Both states use the same page structure and visual language, keeping the context consistent as the interaction changes.

RESPONSIVE DESIGN

Desktop to mobile.
Keep the hierarchy.

The mobile homepage keeps the gradient, line work and search field, while the navigation becomes compact and the category cards stack vertically.

Typography and spacing change with the viewport. The brand remains recognizable, and the content has room to read.

FINAL DESIGN

One language.
Across the experience.

My end-to-end work connected brand expression, content differentiation and responsive design. The final screens show that system in practice: a strong first impression, clear routes into technical content, and attention to the states in between.
